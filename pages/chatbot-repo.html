What you get

Three tools in one private workspace

Private chat

A chat assistant for lesson planning, differentiation, assessment, communication, and more. Your conversation stays on your computer.

Prompt library

Copy-paste example prompts, each with [BRACKETS] to fill in. Load one and go.

Rubric Grader

Paste a rubric, upload a folder of student work, and get a feedback PDF per student, packed into one download.

The one rule

AI drafts. A human teacher reads, edits, and approves everything before it reaches a student. This tool is a helper, never the grade of record.

Copy-paste ready

Example prompts to get you started

These ship inside the app and can be loaded with one click. Each is a self-contained prompt with [BRACKETS] where you add your specifics.

Grade a whole class set with your rubric

Use this with the Rubric Grader tool: paste it in, upload your rubric and student files, and it writes a feedback PDF for every student.

You are an experienced, fair, and supportive teacher. I am going to give
you a rubric and a folder of student work. Grade EVERY student against the
SAME rubric. For each student, return: (1) a score or level for each rubric
criterion, (2) an overall score, and (3) one or two paragraphs of specific,
encouraging feedback that tells the student exactly what they did well and
the one or two things to work on next. Be honest and do not inflate scores
to be kind. If the work is incomplete or does not address the prompt, say so
plainly. Write feedback in the student's voice level and respect their
dignity. Never claim the student wrote something they did not. You are
drafting feedback for a human teacher to review before it goes to the student.
Open the Rubric Grader

Write a lesson plan from a standard

You are an expert curriculum designer for [GRADE] [SUBJECT]. Design a
complete 50-minute lesson for this standard: [PASTE STANDARD]. Include:
(1) a clear, measurable learning objective; (2) a 5-minute opening hook
that builds curiosity; (3) 15 minutes of direct instruction with concrete
examples and non-examples; (4) a 15-minute guided or independent practice
activity where students DO the thinking (not just watch); (5) a 5-minute
formative check for understanding; and (6) a 5-minute closing and exit
ticket. Differentiate for a student who struggles and a student who is
ready for a stretch. Keep cognitive load low: one clear idea per step. Do
not invent facts or sources. Format the plan with clear headings I can
copy into my planner.

Draft feedback for one student's writing

You are a supportive writing teacher. Here is a piece of student writing:
[PASTE WRITING]. Write honest, specific feedback that (1) names two things
the student did well with specific examples from their text, (2) names ONE
most important thing to improve, with a concrete strategy, and (3) ends on
an encouraging note. Keep it to about 150 words. Match the tone to a
respectful peer coach, not a judge. Do not invent praise or point to things
that are not in the text. This is a draft for me to edit before sharing.

Differentiate an assignment for three readiness levels

Here is an assignment: [PASTE ASSIGNMENT]. Create three versions for the
same learning goal: (A) Support level, for a student who needs more
scaffolding and a smaller cognitive load; (B) Core level, the standard
version; and (C) Stretch level, for a student ready for deeper challenge.
Keep the core learning goal and the assessment of it the same across all
three, so the versions stay fair. For each version, state what stays the
same and what changes and why. Do not lower the bar for the support
version, just change the path. Present the three versions with clear
headings.

Turn a vague assignment into a clear rubric

You are an expert in standards-based grading. Here is an assignment or
learning goal: [PASTE ASSIGNMENT]. Build a clear, usable rubric with 3 to 5
criteria. For each criterion, give 4 performance levels with descriptions
written in language a student can understand: Exceeding, Meeting,
Approaching, and Not yet. Make each level description specific and
observable, not vague adjectives. Then add a one-line note on how I might
give useful feedback when a student lands at each level. Do not invent
standards or requirements beyond what I gave you.

Build a quiz from your notes or a chapter

You are a test writer for [GRADE] [SUBJECT]. Using the material below,
create a 10-question assessment: 4 multiple-choice questions, 3 short-answer
questions, 2 application questions that ask students to use the idea in a
new situation, and 1 question that asks students to explain their reasoning.
Provide an answer key for the multiple choice and a rubric-style answer key
for the constructed responses. Keep language clear and fair, avoid trick
questions, and make sure every question is answerable from the material I
give you. Here is the material: [PASTE NOTES OR CHAPTER].

Write a parent email that is warm and clear

You are a warm, professional teacher writing to a parent. Draft a short
email (under 180 words) that: (1) opens with a positive, specific
observation about the student, (2) states one area for growth honestly but
kindly, (3) proposes a next step and invites partnership, and (4) ends with
warmth and an open door. Fill in these details: student [NAME], subject
[SUBJECT], the positive [OBSERVATION], the concern [CONCERN], and the
suggested next step [NEXT STEP]. Do not invent information I did not give
you. Write in a voice I can send after light editing. Offer 2 slightly
different openings and let me pick.

Two ways to run it

Choose your AI engine

Local Ollama: free and offline

Ollama runs on your computer. Nothing leaves the machine. This is the strongest privacy option and costs nothing.

  • Install the Ollama app, pull a small model.
  • Fast, private, works without internet.
  • Best for a school computer or a classroom.

Ollama Cloud: works without installing a model

Connect your free Ollama Cloud account with an API key. No model download, easy setup, works on any machine.

  • Free tier available; you set your own budget.
  • Needs internet and an account.
  • Great way to start, or to put a public demo online.
Recommendation

Start with Ollama Cloud if you want the fastest setup. Use local Ollama if you want the strongest privacy or you are often offline. You can switch later by changing one line in the .env file.

Step 1 · Get the files

Download or clone the repository

The whole app lives in one folder. You can clone it with git, or download it as a zip.

Download the zip

  1. Go to the repository page on GitHub.
  2. Click the green Code button.
  3. Choose Download ZIP.
  4. Unzip it into a folder you can find.

Or clone with git

git clone https://github.com/YOUR-USER/ai-teacher-workbench.git ai-workbench
cd ai-workbench

Step 2 · Install Python

Install Python (one time, all systems)

The app is written in Python. You need Python 3.9 or newer. It is free.

Windows

  1. Go to python.org/downloads and download the installer.
  2. Run it. On the first screen, tick the box "Add Python to PATH". This is essential.
  3. Click Install Now.

If you skip "Add Python to PATH", the setup script will not find Python. Reinstall and tick the box.

macOS

  1. Go to python.org/downloads.
  2. Download the macOS installer and run it.
  3. Follow the prompts to install.

Linux (Ubuntu / Debian)

Open a terminal and run:

sudo apt update
sudo apt install python3 python3-venv python3-pip

Step 3 · Pick your AI engine

Set up Ollama (local) or Ollama Cloud

Local Ollama, free and fully offline

  1. Go to ollama.com/download and install the app for your system.
  2. Open a terminal (Command Prompt on Windows, Terminal on macOS/Linux) and pull a small model:
ollama pull llama3.2

Leave the Ollama app running. It works in the background. Other good small models: qwen2.5, gemma2, llama3.1. A powerful computer can run llama3.3 or mistral.

Ollama Cloud, free tier or paid

  1. Go to ollama.com/signup and create a free account.
  2. In your dashboard, create an API key.
  3. Copy the key. You will paste it into the .env file in the next step.

Ollama Cloud has a free tier. You can set your own budget in the dashboard. The app never spends anything unless you point it at your key.

Step 4 · Run the one-time setup

Install the app (first time only)

The setup script creates a private Python environment and installs the few libraries it needs. It also creates your .env file from a template.

Windows

Double-click setup.bat, or open Command Prompt in the folder and run:

setup.bat

macOS / Linux

Open Terminal in the folder and run:

chmod +x setup.sh run.sh
./setup.sh

Step 5 · Configure

Everything lives in one .env file

Open the .env file in any text editor. It is a simple list of NAME=value lines. This one file controls everything.

For local Ollama

Make sure the first line reads:

PROVIDER=ollama

That is it. Leave the API key blank.

For Ollama Cloud

Change the first line and add your key:

PROVIDER=cloud
OLLAMA_API_KEY=your-key-here
Keep your key private

The .env file is automatically ignored by git, so your key is never committed. Never rename it, and never paste your key into a chat, email, or shared file.

Step 6 · Start the app

Run it and open it in your browser

Double-click run.bat, or run:

run.bat

Run:

./run.sh

Open http://localhost:5000 in your browser. You will see the workbench with a status box that tells you whether your AI engine is ready.

If it says "Not ready"

Local Ollama: the Ollama app must be running, and the model must be pulled (ollama pull llama3.2). Cloud: the API key is missing or wrong in .env.

Put it to work

Grade a class set with the Rubric Grader

  1. Open the Rubric Grader tab

    It is the second tab at the top of the app.

  2. Paste your rubric

    A rubric with criteria and performance levels works best, for example: "Criterion 1: Thesis. Exceeding: a clear, arguable, insightful thesis. Meeting: clear and arguable. Approaching: present but vague. Not yet: no thesis." Repeat for each criterion.

  3. Upload one file per student

    Name each file with the student's name, such as jamie-smith.docx. Supported: PDF, Word (.docx), plain text (.txt), Markdown (.md), CSV (.csv).

  4. Generate feedback PDFs

    The app reads each file, scores it against the rubric, and writes a feedback PDF per student into the output folder, offered as one download.

  5. Review every PDF

    Open each one, edit anything you disagree with, and approve it before it goes to a student. The AI is a drafting assistant, not the grader of record.

Privacy & safety

Where your data goes

Local Ollama

Everything stays on your device. Nothing leaves the machine. This is the strongest privacy option.

Ollama Cloud

Your prompts are sent to Ollama's API. Ollama does not train on API data, but content leaves your device, so keep it to lesson content, not unnecessary student PII.

Student data

Never paste real student names, grades, medical info, or other personally identifying information beyond what the assignment itself needs. The district AI policy still applies: confirm any tool against your school or district approved-software list before use.

AI can be biased and can be wrong. Every output you give a student or family must be reviewed by you. You are responsible for the final feedback.
