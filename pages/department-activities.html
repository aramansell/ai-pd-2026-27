ALL YEAR · DEPARTMENT WORK

Department Activities for the Whole Year

The three PD sessions are the launch. This calendar keeps the learning alive in your own department meetings — short, collaborative, and practical. Do one a month, or pick the ones that fit.

⏱ 10–20 min each In your own meetings 2026–27

How this works

One small activity, one department meeting, each month

Built to fit

Each activity fits in 10–20 minutes, so it works inside an agenda — no extra PD time needed.

Collaborative

Activities are designed for sharing, not solo work. Your department's collective expertise is the point.

Cumulative

They build on each other and on the three PD sessions. Do them in order for the full arc.

Month by month

The 2026–27 activity calendar

Goal: Turn Session 1's workflows into a shared department resource.

  1. Each teacher brings one AI prompt they used or want to use (from Session 1's toolkit).
  2. Share them around; each person improves one other person's prompt to fit your subject.
  3. Compile the best into a shared Department Prompt Bank (Google Doc).
  4. Agree to add to it all year.

Takeaway: a living, subject-specific prompt library your department owns.

Goal: Go beyond generic prompts to what's unique about your subject.

  1. Brainstorm: where in your subject does AI save real time or improve learning?
  2. Each teacher commits to one subject-specific AI workflow to try before the next meeting.
  3. Bring back a sample — a differentiated task, a rubric, a feedback comment.

Takeaway: subject-specific AI workflows, tried and shared.

Goal: Make sure your department's AI use protects student data.

  1. Review the privacy rules together.
  2. Audit your department's current AI use: are any student details going into unapproved tools?
  3. Create a short department checklist of safe practices (e.g., "remove names before pasting").
  4. Assign one person to keep the checklist current.

Takeaway: a department privacy checklist everyone follows.

Goal: Share the classroom activities everyone designed in Session 2.

  1. Each teacher brings the activity they adapted in Session 2.
  2. Walk through it with a partner using the cognitive-load test (who's thinking? what's the student's job?).
  3. Give one concrete suggestion to improve each activity.
  4. Collect the best into a Department Activity Library.

Takeaway: a shared library of vetted, subject-ready classroom AI activities.

Goal: Ensure AI access and benefit are fair across your students.

  1. Discuss: who in your classes is using AI? Who isn't? Who's using it well?
  2. Review the tools you're using — do they cost money? Do they work on Chromebooks? Are they available at home?
  3. Plan one adjustment to close an access gap (e.g., a free tool, a class account, an offline option).

Takeaway: a concrete equity improvement your department commits to.

Goal: Teach bias awareness using your own content.

  1. Design one bias investigation using AI in your subject (use the "Who's in the picture?" model from Session 3).
  2. Two teachers teach it; the rest observe.
  3. Debrief: what did students notice? How did the conversation go?

Takeaway: a tested bias lesson you can all reuse.

Goal: Roll out student AI literacy consistently across your department.

  1. Agree on the "Ask, Check, Decide" process as your shared approach.
  2. Each teacher runs their AI-literacy mini-lesson (from Session 3).
  3. Compare: what worked? Where did students struggle?
  4. Refine the shared lesson for next year.

Takeaway: consistent AI-literacy instruction across all your classes.

Goal: Align your department's expectations for student AI use.

  1. Share the class agreements everyone created in Session 3.
  2. Find common ground: what's the same across classes? What differs?
  3. Draft a department-wide statement of allowed vs. not-allowed student AI use.
  4. Use it to inform the school policy.

Takeaway: a consistent, department-wide honest-work standard.

Goal: Capture your department's learning for next year.

  1. Review everything you created: prompts, activities, lessons, policies.
  2. Curate the best into a tidy Department AI Handbook.
  3. Note what you'd do differently next year.
  4. Hand the handbook to next year's team (and add it to this site's Resources).

Takeaway: your department's legacy, ready for next year.

For department leads

Facilitator tips

Keep it short & practical

Resist making these bigger than 20 minutes. The point is momentum, not perfection. Small wins build confidence.

Share the load

Rotate who leads each month's activity so no one person carries it. Everyone has something to contribute.

Capture everything

Keep one shared Google Doc or Drive folder for your department's prompts, activities, and agreements. Don't lose the work.

Connect to the sessions

Each activity names which PD session it builds on. If a colleague missed a session, point them to the session page.
