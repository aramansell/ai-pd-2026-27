The district standard

PPS "Levels of Use" for student AI

The PPS AI Guidebook defines three levels of AI use for student work. The instructor decides which level applies to each assignment. Use this as the consistent language across your classroom and department.

Restrictive · AI not allowed

AI tools should NOT be used. Work must be completely original and reflect the student's own effort, including planning or drafting where originality is paramount. These assignments assess specific skills, knowledge, and original expression.

!
Moderate · AI allowed with citation

AI CAN be used for specific parts of the assignment, but the student MUST give credit to the AI tool and the content it provided. Good for research, brainstorming, and initial drafting.

Permissive · AI allowed without citation

AI CAN be used freely without formal citation. These tasks focus on skills where AI use doesn't impact the originality of the core work or assessment of essential skills, e.g., aggregating existing information.

Adapted from the PPS AI Guidebook (after Tigard-Tualatin SD). Teachers state the level for each assignment. The Classroom Agreement template helps you co-create the expectations with students.

Modeling transparency

Citing your own AI use (APA)

The PPS Guidebook asks staff to model academic integrity by citing their own AI use, when creating materials or policies informed by AI, and for any generative AI used in PPS business, communications, instruction, or training. Here's the APA format PPS provides:

Format: AI Company Name. (year, month day). Title of chat [Generative AI chat]. Tool Name/Model. URL of the chat

Example: Google. (2025, May 22). High school grammar concepts overview [Generative AI chat]. Gemini 2.5 Flash. https://g.co/gemini/share/a1306ce12929

Disclose AI use whenever possible. It's a small step that builds a culture of honest, responsible use across the school.

For your students

Classroom "Honest Work" agreement

A short, student-friendly agreement to co-create with your class. Keep it simple, students should be able to explain each line in their own words.

Our Class AI Agreement

  • Transparency: I'll tell my teacher when and how I used AI.
  • Thinking: I'll use AI as an assistant, never to do my thinking for me.
  • Verification: I'll check AI output against trustworthy sources.
  • Privacy: I'll never type my name, school, or personal information into AI.
  • Asking: If I'm not sure AI use is allowed, I'll ask my teacher.

Write a better policy

Tips for a policy people actually follow

Say what students CAN do

List allowed uses clearly. Students follow policies that feel like permission, not just a list of crimes.

Make it subject-specific

"Use AI to brainstorm" means different things in English vs. math. Department policies handle this.

Review it twice a year

AI changes fast. Plan to revisit your policy in winter and spring.

Co-create with students

Students who help write the policy are far more likely to follow it.
