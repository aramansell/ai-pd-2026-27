PRIVACY & STUDENT DATA

The plain-language rules for protecting student data

When you paste something into an AI tool, where does it go? Who can see it? Does it train a model? This page gives you simple, actionable rules — no legalese.

🔒 FERPA basics 🚫 What never to paste ✅ What's safe

The one rule to remember

If you wouldn't put it on the hallway bulletin board, don't put it in an AI tool

✅
Safe to paste (with names removed)

Lesson topics · subject content · anonymized assignment prompts · standards text · generic feedback phrasing · a rubric. The moment you remove names and identifiers, the risk drops dramatically.

🚫
Never paste into an unapproved tool

Full student names · grades · IEP/504 details · medical, family, or discipline info · photos · voice recordings · email addresses · student ID numbers · anything that identifies a student.

The law in plain English

What FERPA means for your AI use

FERPA (the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act) protects students' education records. Here's what that means in practice when using AI:

📋 What counts as a "record"

Grades, transcripts, IEPs, discipline records, and any documentation that identifies a student. Putting these into an AI tool that isn't approved for student data can be a FERPA problem.

🔑 The "school official" test

Data can flow to a "school official with a legitimate educational interest." Consumer AI tools generally don't meet that standard unless the district has a contract (DPA) with them. If there's no contract, treat the tool as not approved for student data.

💡
The good news

Removing identifying information ("de-identifying") means it's usually no longer a student record. That's why our #1 rule is: remove names before you paste.

The big question

"Does this tool train on my data?"

This is the most important distinction. The answer varies by tool and by plan (free vs paid). See the Free vs Paid Plans page for the full breakdown. In general:

Situation Typical default What it means
Free consumer AI (no account / personal account) Often trains Your conversations may be used to train the model. Do not put student data here.
Paid consumer plans Usually doesn't train Most paid plans state they don't train on your conversations. Still avoid identifiable student data unless approved.
School / education plans Doesn't train Contractually protected. This is the safe way to use AI with student data.
Local AI (runs on your device) Stays on device Ollama and similar run entirely on your machine. Data never leaves your device.

Before you paste

The 10-second decision

  1. Is there identifiable student info?

    Names, grades, IEP details, anything personal? If yes, remove it first.

  2. Is the tool approved for student data?

    Check the approved list. If it's a school/district account or a local tool, you're generally safe.

  3. Is the free tier going to train on it?

    If it's a free consumer account and might train, don't paste student data — period.

  4. When in doubt, ask

    Ask your department lead or IT before trying something new. Better to ask than to put a student at risk.

Equity & access

Privacy is also an equity issue

If some students' parents pay for premium AI and others can't, that's an equity gap. And every family has a right to know how their child's data is (and isn't) used.

🗣️
Communicate with families

Let families know which AI tools you use with students, what data (if any) is involved, and how to opt out.

⚖️
Prefer free, approved tools

Using free, district-approved tools means every student can participate regardless of what their family can afford.
