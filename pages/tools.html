PPS guidance

How PPS wants us to use AI

PPS Vision

"Portland Public Schools will leverage Artificial Intelligence (AI) as a transformative, ethical, and human-centered tool to accelerate student achievement, disrupt systemic inequities, and prepare every graduate for an AI-powered future." PPS views AI as a tool to support and enhance human work, not replace it.

The district encourages the intentional, ethical, and safe exploration of AI to amplify creativity and personalize learning, while upholding commitments to equity and data privacy. Use of AI is governed by the district's Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) and Administrative Directive 8.60.041-AD.

Staff can use approved AI for

Learning Design, generating lesson ideas, practice problems, and differentiated materials while teaching to the curriculum with integrity.
Administrative Tasks, drafting emails and communications.
Instructional Support, finding resources and providing preliminary translations.

Always

Human Oversight, review all AI-generated content for accuracy, bias, and appropriateness.
Transparency, model academic integrity by citing your use of AI when creating professional materials.

Student data

Never upload student work, grades, or Personally Identifiable Information (PII) into AI tools unless the district has specifically authorized it and a Data Privacy Agreement (DPA) is in place.

AI detectors

PPS staff should not rely on AI detection tools, as they are currently unreliable and have shown bias against non-native English speakers.

Source: Artificial Intelligence in PPS and the PPS AI Guidebook.

PPS-approved AI tools for staff

These tools are vetted and approved by PPS for staff use with district accounts. When you use them with your PPS login, your data is not used to train AI models.

Tool What it does Good for Student data Cost District
Gemini (Google) Google's assistant, connected to Workspace (Docs, Gmail, Slides). PPS encourages staff to use Gemini with district accounts, enterprise-grade security, no training on your data. Planning, drafting, summarizing, brainstorming inside Google. Safe with district account Free + paid Approved
Adobe Express Design tool with generative AI, flyers, graphics, social posts, presentations. Quick visual content; AI image/text effects. Safe with district account Free + paid Approved
Canva (for Education) Design tool with AI (Magic Studio), posters, slides, student projects. Student projects, presentations, visual lessons. Safe with edu account Free for educators* Approved
Kami PDF / document annotation with AI, marking up docs, worksheets, feedback. Digital annotation, differentiated worksheets, feedback on documents. Safe with district account Free + paid Approved
Screencastify Screen recording / video with AI, record lessons, add captions, quizzes. Flipped content, explainer videos, student video responses. Safe with district account Free + paid Approved

Per the PPS approved-tools list for staff. Verify account access through your PPS login. Approval status and pricing change often; last verified August 2026.

Other tools we mention in PD

A few additional tools are approved for teacher use or currently under district review.

Tool What it does Good for Cost District
NotebookLM (Google) Grounded AI workspace, upload sources, get answers and summaries only from what you uploaded. Zero-hallucination research and study guides from your own materials. Free Approved for teacher use
Ollama (local) Runs open-source AI models on your own device. No cloud, no account. Complete privacy; learning how AI works; simple tasks offline. Free Approved for teacher use
Diffit Differentiates any text to multiple reading levels and standards. Adapting reading material for varied levels & EL students. Free + paid Approved for teacher use
SchoolAI "Spaces" where students work with AI under teacher control. Structured, monitored student AI activities. Free trial + paid Approved for teacher use
Claude (Anthropic) Anthropic's assistant, known for thoughtful writing and strong analysis of long documents (large context window, extended thinking). Drafting and editing nuanced prose; analyzing long documents and PDFs; careful lesson planning; code and data work. Free + paid Under review
Claude for Education (Anthropic) Anthropic's dedicated institutional plan for a school's students, faculty, and staff. Discounted access for all school members; academic research & learning mode with dedicated API credits; training and enablement. Institutional Under review
ChatGPT for teachers (OpenAI) Teacher-specific ChatGPT offering from OpenAI. Planning, feedback, and general tasks if approved. Paid Under review
Microsoft Copilot / M365 AI assistant inside Microsoft 365 apps. Drafting in Word/Outlook/Teams if your M365 plan includes it. Varies Under review

Pick the right tool

Which approved tool should I reach for?

I want to plan or write

Start with Gemini (in your Google account). For research grounded in your own sources, add NotebookLM.

I need to differentiate reading

Diffit is purpose-built for adapting text to levels.

I want a monitored student activity

SchoolAI Spaces give you teacher-controlled student AI use.

I want to make visuals

Adobe Express, Canva, or Kami for documents, all approved.

I want to record or flip a lesson

Screencastify for screen recording and video with captions.

I care most about privacy

Ollama runs locally, so nothing leaves your device.

One tool won't do everything

Think of these like physical tools: a hammer, a screwdriver, a level. Use the right one for the job. For the cost breakdown and where student work can go, see the Free vs Paid Plans page.
