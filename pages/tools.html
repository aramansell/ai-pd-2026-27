Compare the AI tools

Category 1 · All-purpose assistants

The big three chatbots + a local option

These general assistants do a little of everything. Use them for planning, writing, brainstorming, and questions. They're the most flexible tools in the box.

Gemini Free + paid

Google's all-purpose assistant. Deeply connected to Google Workspace (Docs, Gmail, Slides).

Good for
  • Drafting docs, emails, slides inside Google
  • Summarizing and searching your Google Drive
  • Brainstorming and quick research
Not great for
  • Long, nuanced analytical writing (Claude often wins)
  • Being fully private — consumer free tier may use your chats
Student data

Caution Teacher use without student PII is fine. For student data, only use a school/district Workspace-for-Education setup that's approved.

Cost

Free tier; Google AI Plus ~$10/mo, Pro ~$20/mo. See Plans.

Claude Free + paid

Anthropic's assistant. Widely praised for thoughtful writing, analysis, and long documents.

Good for
  • Drafting and editing nuanced prose
  • Analyzing long documents and PDFs
  • Careful, well-structured lesson planning
Not great for
  • Native Google Workspace integration (Gemini wins there)
  • Free tier has usage limits; gets slower during peak
Student data

Caution Fine for teacher use without student PII. Anthropic offers education/institutional plans — use an approved school account for student data.

Cost

Free tier; Team from ~$20/seat/mo. See Plans.

ChatGPT Free + paid

OpenAI's assistant. The most widely known; strong at conversation, code, and general tasks.

Good for
  • General questions and quick drafting
  • Coding and problem-solving help
  • Generating ideas and practice material
Not great for
  • Very long, complex documents (can lose the thread)
  • Being private on the free tier
Student data

Caution Teacher use without student PII is fine. For student data, use ChatGPT for Education / an approved school account — never a personal free account.

Cost

Free tier; Plus and Team are paid. See Plans.

Ollama Free Local

Runs open-source AI models directly on your own computer or Chromebook. No cloud, no account.

Good for
  • Complete privacy — data never leaves your device
  • Learning how AI actually works
  • Simple tasks on school hardware with no sign-in
Not great for
  • Large, complex, or fast tasks (smaller models are weaker)
  • Needs a reasonably powerful device
  • Chromebooks can be limited for bigger models
Student data

Safe Stays on your device — the most private option.

Cost

Free, open source.

Category 2 · Built for teachers' daily work

Time-saving tools for planning, differentiation & feedback

These are purpose-built to save teachers hours. Great first stop after the big chatbots.

Tool What it does Good for Not great for Student data Cost
MagicSchool Lesson plans, quizzes, rubrics, feedback — dozens of AI "tools" for teachers. Fast lesson & assessment generation; student-facing tools. May feel templated; verify output. Safe (edu) Free + paid
Brisk Teaching AI that lives inside Google Docs and Slides — creates, differentiates, gives feedback. Working where you already do; differentiation; feedback. Best inside Google tools; less standalone. Safe (edu) Free + paid
Diffit Differentiates any text to multiple reading levels and standards. Adapting reading material for varied levels & EL students. Best for text-based differentiation. Safe (edu) Free ~$15/mo paid
Khanmigo (Khan Academy) AI tutor + teacher assistant from Khan Academy. Free teacher tools; tutoring for learners. Learner version has a fee; verify coverage. Safe (edu) Free for teachers

Category 3 · Interactive lessons & student engagement

Tools for live, teacher-supervised classroom use

Tool What it does Good for Not great for Student data Cost
Curipod Interactive lessons with AI — polls, quizzes, and responses. Engaging whole-class activities; quick checks. Best as a supplement, not the whole lesson. Safe (edu) Free + paid
SchoolAI "Spaces" where students work with AI under teacher control. Structured, monitored student AI activities. Requires setup; usage caps on free trial. Safe (edu) Free trial + paid
Canva (for Education) Design tool with AI features (Magic Studio). Student projects, posters, presentations. AI features vary by plan; verify educator free access. Safe (edu) Free for educators*

Pick the right tool

Which tool should I reach for?

📝 I want to plan or write

Start with Claude or Gemini. For lesson plans fast, add MagicSchool or Brisk.

📖 I need to differentiate reading

Diffit is purpose-built for adapting text to levels. Brisk does it inside Google Docs.

🎓 I want a monitored student activity

SchoolAI Spaces or Curipod give you teacher-controlled student AI use.

🔒 I care most about privacy

Ollama runs locally, so nothing leaves the device. Best for sensitive work.

💻 I'm on a Chromebook at school

All the web tools above work in a browser. For local AI on a Chromebook, keep expectations modest.

👩‍🎓 I want to tutor students

Khanmigo (Khan Academy) offers an AI tutor that's free for teachers.

💡
One tool won't do everything

Think of these like physical tools — a hammer, a screwdriver, a level. Use the right one for the job. And for the cost breakdown + which plans are safe for student work, see the Free vs Paid Plans page.
